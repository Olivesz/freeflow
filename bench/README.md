# Streaming benchmark

Does streaming cost accuracy? Each clip is decoded twice with the same model
(whisper.cpp `base.en`): once whole, offline, and once replayed at the app's
350 ms tick through the same `StreamingSession` the mac app runs. Both
transcripts are scored against the reference.

## Run it

```bash
mac/fetch_model.sh          # base.en + Core ML encoder, sha256-checked
bench/run.sh                # the shipped settings, all 41 clips
bench/run.sh beam --beam    # any other label + Freeflow --bench flags
python3 bench/table.py bench/results/<commit>/<label>
python3 bench/ticks.py bench/results/<commit>/<label>/logs/jfk.jsonl
```

`run.sh` refuses to run on uncommitted code, so every directory under
`results/` is named for the commit that produced it. A full run took about
9 minutes on an M5 that was also running other work (load average around 10).

## Clips

`manifest.json`, 41 clips, 392 s, 1,036 reference words:

- `clips/jfk.wav`: the whisper.cpp sample (John F. Kennedy, 1961; public
  domain).
- `clips/librispeech/`: 40 utterances from LibriSpeech test-clean (CC BY 4.0,
  [openslr.org/12](https://www.openslr.org/12)), copied byte for byte with
  their sha256 in the manifest. `librispeech.py` picks them by a fixed rule:
  for each speaker in ID order, the first utterance between 4 and 20 s.

The first 20 speakers and JFK are the **dev** split, used while finding and
fixing the defect below and for choosing the window. The last 20 speakers are
**held out**: they were first scored after the fix was committed, and play no
part in any choice.

CI replays a fixed subset, marked `gate` in the manifest (JFK and every fifth
speaker), and fails if streaming WER exceeds offline WER by more than 2 points
(`gate.py`). That subset was chosen after the dev runs, so it is a regression
check, not a result.

## Columns (`summary.csv`)

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `stream_wer`, `offline_wer` | word error rate after lowercasing and stripping punctuation (`Scoring.normalize`); numbers are not spelled out |
| `dropped_words` | reference words missing from the streamed transcript (deletions) |
| `lag_p50_ms`, `lag_p95_ms` | per word: when the pass that committed it finished decoding, minus the word's end time |
| `decode_p50_ms`, `decode_p95_ms` | wall time of one decoder pass |
| `rtf` | total decode time over audio time |
| `cpu_per_audio_s` | process CPU seconds over audio seconds |

WER and drops are deterministic: the same commit, model and clips give the
same transcripts. Timing and CPU depend on the machine and what else it is
running; `run.json` records the machine and its load average.

## Results

Generated by `table.py`. WER is corpus WER: all errors over all reference
words in the split. Held-out is 20 speakers and 523 words; dev is 20 speakers
plus JFK, 513 words.

### Before the fix: every window loses words

The streamer at [`0f70dcd`](https://github.com/Olivesz/freeflow/tree/0f70dcd), before
[`262c910`](https://github.com/Olivesz/freeflow/commit/262c910).

| run | window | tick | decoder | held-out: streamed / offline WER | dropped | dev: streamed / offline WER | dropped | all 41 clips: streamed / offline | commit lag p50 / p95 | CPU s per audio s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0f70dcd/shipped` | 3 / 1.5 s | 350 ms | greedy | 19.7% / 5.4% | 78 of 523 | 15.8% / 3.5% | 64 of 513 | 17.8% / 4.4% | 1125 / 2131 ms | 3.99 |
| `0f70dcd/trim-8.0-2.0` | 8 / 2 s | 350 ms | greedy | 15.5% / 5.4% | 59 of 523 | 14.0% / 3.5% | 52 of 513 | 14.8% / 4.4% | 1081 / 2314 ms | 3.94 |
| `0f70dcd/trim-none` | none | 350 ms | greedy | 15.3% / 5.4% | 54 of 523 | 13.8% / 3.5% | 55 of 513 | 14.6% / 4.4% | 1208 / 2529 ms | 4.84 |
| `0f70dcd/beam` | 3 / 1.5 s | 350 ms | beam | 21.4% / 5.0% | 80 of 523 | 17.0% / 3.1% | 65 of 513 | 19.2% / 4.1% | 1197 / 2113 ms | 4.26 |

Offline decoding of the same clips gets 4.1-4.4%, so streaming was adding 10
to 15 points of error, mostly as dropped words. Even with no trimming at all it
lost 109 words, which pointed at the commit rule rather than the window.

**Why.** The pass logs (`logs/*.jsonl`) show it. A new pass's words are kept
only if they start after the last committed word ends, but whisper.cpp
re-times words by up to a second from one pass to the next, and stretches the
last word of a pass toward the window's end. On JFK, "can" moved to start
0.4 s before the end of the committed "you" and was discarded. In
908-31957-0001 one pass ended a word at 30 s in a 2 s window, and every word
after it was discarded. The fix finds the cut by matching the last committed
words in the new pass, clamps times to the window, and trims from the last
word's start.

### After the fix: choosing the window and decoder

The fixed streamer at [`80ec19e`](https://github.com/Olivesz/freeflow/tree/80ec19e). The window and decoder
were chosen on the **dev** columns only: 3 / 1.5 s with greedy decoding has the
lowest dev WER (4.9%). Beam search was never a real candidate: the app moved to
greedy after beam search crashed whisper.cpp mid-dictation (SIGTRAP, exit 133).
It did not crash on these 41 clips; it is here for comparison.

| run | window | tick | decoder | held-out: streamed / offline WER | dropped | dev: streamed / offline WER | dropped | all 41 clips: streamed / offline | commit lag p50 / p95 | CPU s per audio s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `80ec19e/matrix-greedy-3.0-1.5` | 3 / 1.5 s | 350 ms | greedy | 6.7% / 5.4% | 3 of 523 | 4.9% / 3.5% | 2 of 513 | 5.8% / 4.4% | 1118 / 2231 ms | 4.72 |
| `80ec19e/trim-5.0-2.0` | 5 / 2 s | 350 ms | greedy | 6.3% / 5.4% | 7 of 523 | 6.0% / 3.5% | 10 of 513 | 6.2% / 4.4% | 1028 / 2266 ms | 4.20 |
| `80ec19e/trim-8.0-2.0` | 8 / 2 s | 350 ms | greedy | 7.8% / 5.4% | 6 of 523 | 5.5% / 3.5% | 8 of 513 | 6.7% / 4.4% | 965 / 2116 ms | 3.47 |
| `80ec19e/trim-none` | none | 350 ms | greedy | 6.5% / 5.4% | 7 of 523 | 5.3% / 3.5% | 8 of 513 | 5.9% / 4.4% | 906 / 1775 ms | 3.02 |
| `80ec19e/beam` | 3 / 1.5 s | 350 ms | beam | 7.8% / 5.0% | 3 of 523 | 6.0% / 3.1% | 1 of 513 | 6.9% / 4.1% | 928 / 1651 ms | 3.19 |
| `80ec19e/beam-8.0-2.0` | 8 / 2 s | 350 ms | beam | 6.5% / 5.0% | 4 of 523 | 6.0% / 3.1% | 7 of 513 | 6.3% / 4.1% | 940 / 1818 ms | 3.26 |

Read as a sensitivity table, the held-out column says the windows land within
about 1.5 points of each other once the commit rule is fixed: 3 / 1.5 s is not
the best window on every split (5 / 2 s is 0.4 points lower on held-out), and
the choice was not revisited after seeing that.

### Shipped settings, timing

At [`9832030`](https://github.com/Olivesz/freeflow/tree/9832030). `timing` is the run quoted for latency
and CPU; it ran with other work on the machine (load average 3.6 at its start,
`run.json`), so its timing is an upper bound for an idle machine. WER is
identical to `80ec19e` and to `9832030/shipped`, which ran at load 8.

| run | window | tick | decoder | held-out: streamed / offline WER | dropped | dev: streamed / offline WER | dropped | all 41 clips: streamed / offline | commit lag p50 / p95 | CPU s per audio s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `9832030/timing` | 3 / 1.5 s | 350 ms | greedy | 6.7% / 5.4% | 3 of 523 | 4.9% / 3.5% | 2 of 513 | 5.8% / 4.4% | 787 / 1557 ms | 1.65 |
| `9832030/tick-600` | 3 / 1.5 s | 600 ms | greedy | 6.7% / 5.4% | 8 of 523 | 5.5% / 3.5% | 2 of 513 | 6.1% / 4.4% | 1184 / 2119 ms | 1.15 |

- **Commit lag** is how long after a word ends it is typed: a median of
  0.79 s at the app's 350 ms tick against 1.18 s at the Python app's old
  600 ms tick. A word needs two agreeing passes, so the tick sets most of it.
- **Decode** takes 165 ms per pass at the median and 353 ms at p95
  (`summary.csv`, ALL row), so at a 350 ms tick the decoder keeps up with
  real time on most passes. The replay feeds audio at a fixed 350 ms per tick
  regardless of decode time; live, a slow pass delays the next tick instead.
- **CPU**: 1.65 CPU-seconds per second of audio, spread over whisper.cpp's
  threads, with the Core ML encoder loaded (`run.json`).
